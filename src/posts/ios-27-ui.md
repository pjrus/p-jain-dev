I'll say it plainly: iOS 27 has the best UI Apple has shipped in years. Not "fine, I'm used to it now" — actually good.

## Liquid Glass stopped being a gimmick

iOS 26 introduced Liquid Glass and it felt like a tech demo — translucent panels everywhere, contrast fighting legibility, glass for the sake of glass. iOS 27 is the version where Apple figured out restraint. The material now reacts to what's behind it instead of just sitting on top and looking pretty. Sheets, control center, notifications — they all feel like they're made of the same substance instead of a dozen teams independently deciding what "glass" means this week.

## First-party apps finally agree with each other

This is the one that actually matters day to day. For years, opening Settings, then Music, then Files felt like using three different products stitched together by a merger. iOS 27 is the first release where that friction is basically gone. Same spacing rules, same control shapes, same interaction language. It sounds boring written down, but it's the thing that makes an OS feel considered instead of assembled.

## Navigation clicked

The tab bar and app switcher changes get the most attention, and they deserve it. Controls float where your thumb already is instead of where a decade-old layout convention put them. Switching between apps and reaching for the thing you actually want to tap both feel shorter than they used to. It's a small physical distance, but it adds up over a day of unlocking your phone 80 times.

## The takeaway

None of this is flashy in a screenshot. It's not a new gesture you'll show a friend. It's the accumulation of a hundred small decisions that all point the same direction, and after a couple of Liquid Glass release cycles that felt more like beta software than a finished product, iOS 27 is the one where it stopped looking like a work in progress and started looking like a decision.
